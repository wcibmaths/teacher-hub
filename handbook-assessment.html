Assessment
Assessment is designed to promote deep understanding, identify individual learning needs and prepare students for external examination. We assess three dimensions each reporting cycle: Current Attainment Grade, Learning Behaviour and Progress Colour.

  • The accelerated pathway now reports on the 9 to 1 scale for 4PM1. The A* to G scale used for Cambridge 0606 no longer applies.
  • KS5 attainment is reported using the A Level scale, A* to E, not 9 to 1.

Key Stage 3

Students are assessed against department-defined expectations using a fine-grained system that captures both conceptual understanding and procedural fluency. Assessments include short quizzes, investigations and end-of-unit tests with a mix of calculation, reasoning and problem-solving questions. Judgements are based on multiple data points, never a single test.

Key Stage 4

CAGs are awarded in line with official exam board grade boundaries and expectations. Teachers base judgements on mock papers, structured past-paper tasks, end-of-topic assessments and the mathematical reasoning demonstrated in class.

Grading reflects where a student is currently performing, not a predicted outcome. Teachers must be able to justify every grade with evidence from classwork, assessments or platform diagnostics.

Key Stage 5

CAGs are awarded in line with official Edexcel A Level standards, based on how students are currently performing against A Level criteria using evidence from modular content taught to date:

  • Past-paper questions under timed conditions
  • Topic and modular assessments marked using official mark schemes
  • Full mock exams
  • In-class problem solving, reasoning tasks and independent assignments

Teachers apply professional judgement to evaluate how securely students have grasped key concepts and how fluently they apply them in increasingly complex contexts. Grades must be supported by clear assessment evidence and moderated within the department where necessary.

Learning Behaviour and Progress Colour

Learning Behaviour

Graded 1 (Excellent) to 3 (Inconsistent) using consistent whole-school criteria, adapted to reflect expectations at each stage.

Progress Colour

Reflects whether a student is on track to meet or exceed their end-of-year target grade. Teachers weigh effort, test performance, consistency and pace of improvement when assigning a colour.

Departmental practice

1

Purpose of assessment

To diagnose what students understand and where misconceptions lie, to inform instruction, pacing and intervention, and to communicate progress clearly to students, parents and stakeholders. Assessment is a process embedded in classroom practice, not a single event.

2

Evidence-based judgement

All CAGs must rest on verifiable evidence: end-of-topic tests, timed assessments, mocks and past-paper questions, and classwork or structured prep. Teachers keep clear records of key assessments including scores, marked scripts and notes. Grades reflect demonstrated performance, not effort, behaviour or potential.

3

Design and alignment

Summative assessments mirror the structure and challenge of external qualifications. KS3 uses KS4-style question types scaled in complexity, KS4 follows IGCSE formats with increasing past-paper use by Year 11, and KS5 follows the Edexcel modular structure. Each assessment balances procedural fluency, conceptual reasoning and problem solving in unfamiliar contexts.

4

Moderation and consistency

Internal moderation takes place before each data drop, particularly for borderline cases, mocks and final internal assessments, and any group taught by different teachers using the same assessment. Moderation includes review of marked scripts, cross-referencing exemplars and professional discussion. No CAG is submitted without at least two pieces of supporting evidence.

Marking and feedback

  • Assessments are marked using shared departmental mark schemes or official exam board rubrics, applied consistently across classes and year levels.
  • Common grade boundaries are agreed in advance for mocks, modular tests and shared unit tests.
  • Feedback must be timely, specific and actionable, not simply evaluative.
  • Where written marking is used it must serve a clear instructional purpose and lead to student action.

Approved strategies

  • Whole-class feedback sheets
  • Re-teach tasks based on common errors
  • Modelling of exemplar responses
  • Student corrections and reflective reworking of solutions
Core principles of effective feedback
  • It improves learning rather than documenting it
  • It is only valuable if students act on it
  • It does not require written comments to have impact
  • It is timely, specific and actionable
  • It is sustainable across teacher workload

Department expectations

  • Assessment feedback on tests and mocks must include at least one student response activity.
  • Classwork and prep feedback can be verbal, coded or written. Student action is the focus.
  • Tracking. Use your planner or class lists to check when students have responded.

Monitoring

  • The HoD periodically samples books or online folders for evidence of feedback impact, not volume.
  • The focus is student improvement, not marking frequency.
  • Teachers may be asked to share successful approaches at department meetings.

Professional accountability

  • Every teacher is responsible for ensuring assessment judgements are fair, transparent and documented.
  • Where a student underperforms or a grade is disputed, staff must be able to justify the grade with reference to marked work and departmental criteria.
  • Grades may be reviewed in discussion with the Head of Department before finalisation.
